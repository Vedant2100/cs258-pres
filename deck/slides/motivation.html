Problem: learning only from logged data

Offline RL: learn a policy from a fixed dataset, with no new interaction with the environment.

Real-world example: a hospital

You cannot experiment on patients, but you have records of past treatments and outcomes. Offline RL asks: which treatment next?

Why TD learning struggles offline

  • Bootstrapped targets reuse the model’s own estimates, so errors compound.
  • Values of actions the data never tried get overestimated.
  • Sparse, delayed rewards propagate slowly through Bellman backups.

About 1 minute. "Offline RL means learning a policy only from a fixed log, with no new interaction. A hospital is the clearest case: you can't experiment on patients, but you do have records of past treatments and outcomes. Standard TD methods struggle here: they bootstrap, training value estimates on their own estimates, so errors compound; they overrate actions the data never tried; and sparse, delayed rewards travel slowly through Bellman backups. In lecture terms, offline Q-learning has all three parts of the deadly triad: function approximation, bootstrapping and off-policy data. The paper explicitly avoids bootstrapping for that reason. Keep one picture in mind for the talk: in TD learning, credit crawls backward, one backup at a time." Note: the hospital is an illustrative scenario; the paper itself does not run medical experiments.
